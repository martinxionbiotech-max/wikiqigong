# WikiQigong

**The clear English guide to Tai Chi, Qigong, and Chinese mind–body
practices.**

Chinese culture has spent more than two thousand years thinking carefully
about how the body, breath, and mind work together. That knowledge is deep,
practical, and often lost in translation. WikiQigong exists to bridge the
gap: we take genuine Chinese understanding and express it in **natural,
accurate English** — for people with zero Chinese background, and for
practitioners who want the real thing rather than the mystified version.

---

## What this site is

- **A learning resource**, not a content farm. Every page is a long-term
  asset, written slowly and reviewed carefully.
- **Accurate about Chinese culture.** Every concept carries its characters,
  pinyin, literal meaning, and honest history — we do not strip out the
  Chinese context, and we do not dress it up in mysticism.
- **Honest about evidence.** We clearly separate historical fact, traditional
  interpretation, and modern scientific understanding. Where research exists,
  we cite it; where it is thin, we say so.

## What this site is *not*

- **Not a medical resource.** We use careful wording ("may support," "has been
  studied for"). Nothing here is medical advice, and no practice described
  here cures or treats disease.
- **Not a religion.** These practices have philosophical roots, but they
  require no belief system to learn or benefit from.
- **Not machine translation.** This is deliberate reconstruction of meaning,
  not word-for-word rendering.

## The knowledge graph at a glance

Every concept on this site is connected. Here is the core of the map:

| Core concepts | The "three treasures" | The "three regulations" |
|---------------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| [Qi](glossary/qi.md) | [Jing](glossary/jing.md) → [Qi](glossary/qi.md) → [Shen](glossary/shen.md) | Body · Breath · Mind |
| [Dantian](glossary/dantian.md) | essence → energy → spirit | 調身 · 調息 · 調心 |
| [Yin–Yang](glossary/yin-yang.md) | the classical refinement chain | the method of every practice |

Tai chi and qigong are two expressions of the same body of knowledge — one
martial and moving, one health-focused and often still. This site teaches
both, and the philosophy underneath them.
