# Wu Xing

**What it is:** Wu Xing (五行, *wǔxíng*) is the Chinese framework of the "five
phases" — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water — which extends
[yin–yang](../glossary/yin-yang.md) thinking into a richer cycle of
transformation.

## Literal meaning

**Wu** (五) means "five." **Xing** (行) means "to go, to move, to act" — the
character for "walking." So wuxing is literally "five goings" or "five
movements": not five static *elements* but five *phases* of a cycle.

## Historical context

The five-phase scheme emerged alongside yin–yang in the Warring States and Han
periods as a way to classify natural phenomena — seasons, directions, organs,
emotions — and, crucially, to describe how they *transform into each other*.
It became a core organizing logic of Chinese medicine and cosmology.

## Traditional interpretation

The five phases relate through two cycles: **generation** (Wood feeds Fire,
Fire creates Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal collects Water, Water nourishes
Wood) and **control** (each phase restrains another). Health was traditionally
understood as these cycles in balance.

## Modern explanation

Wu Xing is best read today as an early systems model — a way of describing
interconnected, mutually influencing processes. Its modern value is
conceptual, not physical: it teaches a habit of seeing relationships and
cycles rather than isolated parts.

## Related concepts

- **[Yin–Yang](../glossary/yin-yang.md)** — the framework wuxing extends.
- **[Qi](../glossary/qi.md)** — the vitality that transforms through the
  phases.
- **[Balance](../glossary/balance.md)** — the state the cycles aim to maintain.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"The five phases are literal elements."** They are *phases of a cycle*,
   not physical substances.
2. **"Wu xing is astrology or superstition."** It is a classificatory system
   for relationships and change, not fortune-telling.
3. **"The five phases map onto five literal organs."** In medicine they are
   *functional systems* named after organs, not anatomical claims.
