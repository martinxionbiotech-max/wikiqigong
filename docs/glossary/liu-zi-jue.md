# Liu Zi Jue

**What it is:** Liu Zi Jue (六字訣, *liù zì jué*), the "Six Healing Sounds,"
is a traditional Chinese breathing qigong that pairs six whispered sounds —
*xu, he, hu, si, chui, xi* — with slow, gentle movement, each sound
traditionally linked to a different organ system.

## Literal meaning

**Liu** (六) means "six." **Zi** (字) means "character" or "written word."
**Jue** (訣) means "secret," "formula," or "knack." So the name is literally
"the six-character secret" — six syllables, each treated as a precise
breathing formula passed from teacher to student.

## Historical context

The six-sound method is first clearly described by the Daoist scholar **Tao
Hongjing** (陶弘景, 456–536 CE) in his *Yangxing Yanming Lu* (養性延命錄),
and was later refined by the Tang physician **Sun Simiao** (孫思邈, 581–682
CE), who associated breathing-sound methods with health practice. The
standardized six-sound system taught today was consolidated in the 20th
century.

## Traditional interpretation

In traditional teaching, each of the six sounds is linked to one of the
organ systems of Chinese medicine, arranged through the [Wu Xing](../glossary/wu-xing.md)
framework: *xu* (嘘) to the liver, *he* (呵) to the heart, *hu* (呼) to the
spleen, *si* (呬) to the lungs, *chui* (吹) to the kidneys, and *xi* (嘻) to
the triple burner. The sounds were understood to clear and harmonize the flow
of [qi](../glossary/qi.md) in their associated system.

## Modern explanation

Read in modern terms, Liu Zi Jue is a **breathing-pattern practice**: each
sound is a way to lengthen and shape the exhale, and long, controlled
exhalation *is studied for* its effects on relaxation. The organ associations
are a traditional mnemonic and organizing framework, not anatomical claims;
the practice works as gentle breath and attention training either way.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"The sounds cure specific organs."** They do not. The organ associations
   are a traditional framework; the practice is breath and relaxation
   training, not treatment.
2. **"You must say the sounds loudly."** The opposite — they are whispered,
   long exhales. Loud, forced sounds strain the throat.
3. **"The pronunciation must be exact."** The sounds shape the exhale;
   precision matters less than a slow, comfortable breath.
4. **"It is a religion."** It has Daoist historical roots, but the modern
   practice requires no belief system.
