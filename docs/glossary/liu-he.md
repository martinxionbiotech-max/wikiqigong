# Liu He (Six Harmonies)

**What it is:** The Six Harmonies (六合, *liù hé*) are six pairings — three
internal and three external — that make a movement one coordinated whole rather
than a collection of separate parts.

## Literal meaning

**Liu** (六) is "six." **He** (合) is "to join, to unite, to close" — and the
character carries its own picture: it shows a **mouth** (口) over a **vessel**
or container, the image of a **lid fitting onto a pot** — two things coming
together to seal as one. The six harmonies are thus six *closings*, six places
where two things are joined so exactly they become a single unit: mind and
intention, hands and feet, and so on. The English "harmony" catches the spirit —
voices sounding together as one — but the Chinese "he" is more precise: a
*joining*, a *fitting together*, not merely a pleasant blend.

## Historical context

As a body-method idea, the six harmonies are most strongly associated with the
internal martial arts, especially **Xingyiquan** (形意拳, "Form-Intention
Boxing"), where the three internal pairings (mind–intention, intention–qi,
qi–force) and three external pairings (hands–feet, elbows–knees,
shoulders–hips) are taught as the foundation of whole-body power. The
principle — that the body should move as a coordinated unit — is shared across
Tai Chi, Baguazhang, and Qigong, though different schools state it in different
words. The older cosmological sense of "liu he" as the six directions (cardinal
points plus up and down) predates its body-method use.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally the six harmonies are read as the recipe for whole-body force
(整勁, *zhěng jìn*): no single limb generates power; instead the whole person
unifies. The internal set runs the chain from **mind** (心, *xin*) to
**intention** (意, *yi*) to **qi** (氣) to **force** (力, *li*) — the inside
leading the outside. The external set aligns the skeleton — **hands with feet,
elbows with knees, shoulders with hips** — so the structure can carry that
force. The two sets are the container and what fills it; neither works alone.

## Modern explanation

The six harmonies describe an ordinary principle of skilled movement:
**proximal-to-distal coordination**, where force travels from the ground up
through the legs and trunk to the hands. The external harmonies are a checklist
for that alignment; the internal harmonies describe the role of *attention* —
mind setting an intention, intention guiding the breath and felt quality, and
that quality expressing as relaxed, connected force. No belief in a literal qi
substance is needed: "qi" here is the felt smoothness of an attentive,
well-aligned movement.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"It is six separate rules to memorize."** The six are *relationships* that
   blend into one felt quality of whole-body movement, not a checklist to
   recite.
2. **"The internal harmonies are supernatural energy talk."** Read plainly, they
   describe attention becoming directed movement — an observable process.
3. **"It is only from Xingyiquan."** The name and clearest formulation come
   from the Xingyi tradition, but the principle is shared across the internal
   arts and Qigong.
4. **"It matters only for fighting."** Whole-body coordination — moving from
   the ground up rather than from the shoulder — is central to the gentle,
   joint-friendly quality of Tai Chi and Qigong for health.
