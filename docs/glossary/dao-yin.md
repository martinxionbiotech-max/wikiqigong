# Dao Yin

**What it is:** Dao Yin (導引, *dǎoyǐn*) means "guiding and pulling" — the
ancient Chinese art of therapeutic movement, stretching, and breathing, the
direct ancestor of modern [qigong](../glossary/qigong.md).

## Literal meaning

**Dao** (導) means "to guide, to lead." **Yin** (引) means "to pull, to draw,
to stretch." Together, "guiding and pulling" describes guiding the
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) through the body and pulling/stretching the limbs —
movement and breath working together.

## Historical context

Dao Yin is one of the oldest documented Chinese health practices. Its most
famous artifact is the **Mawangdui silk manuscript**, excavated in 1973 from a
tomb sealed around 168 BCE, which shows painted figures performing stretching
and breathing exercises. The term also appears in the *Huangdi Neijing* (the
Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic), the foundational text of Chinese medicine.
This makes dao yin the direct, traceable ancestor of what is now called
qigong.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, dao yin is understood as a method for keeping the body's qi
moving freely, preventing stagnation that was believed to cause illness.
Practitioners "guide" the breath and attention while "pulling" the body
through postures and stretches — a union of movement, breath, and mind.

## Modern explanation

Dao yin is best read today as **therapeutic movement** — slow, coordinated
stretching and breathing, much like modern qigong or gentle mobility work.
The Mawangdui figures show exactly the kind of gentle, low-impact movements
still practiced today. The "guiding" is the breath and attention; the
"pulling" is the stretch. Nothing supernatural is required.

## Related concepts

- **[Qigong](../glossary/qigong.md)** — the modern umbrella term that grew out
  of dao yin and related practices.
- **[Breathing](../glossary/breathing.md)** — the "guiding" half of the
  practice.
- **[Tai Chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)** — a later movement art on the same
  family tree.
- **[Balance](../glossary/balance.md)** — one of the qualities dao yin
  cultivates.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Dao yin is a specific set of exercises."** It is a *category* of
   therapeutic movement, not one fixed routine.
2. **"Qigong is thousands of years old."** The *word* "qigong" is modern
   (20th century); what is ancient is dao yin and related practices.
3. **"Dao yin requires belief in qi."** The movements and breathing work as
   gentle exercise regardless of framework.
