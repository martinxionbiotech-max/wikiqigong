# Kua (The Hips)

**What it is:** Kua (胯, *kuà*) is the hip and inguinal region — the fold
where the legs meet the torso — that tai chi and qigong treat as the body's
central hinge, asking practitioners to "relax" and "open" it for grounded,
connected movement.

## Literal meaning

**Kua** (胯) names the hip joint and the inguinal crease — the fold at the
front of the body where the thigh meets the trunk. Unlike English "hip,"
which can refer to the bony pelvis, *kua* carries a strong sense of the joint
and fold that allow the legs and torso to move relative to each other.

## Historical context

Kua is a term from the body-awareness vocabulary of the Chinese internal
arts, used for centuries in martial and health training rather than drawn
from a single classical medical text. It became central to tai chi's
teaching as the art formalized in the 17th–19th centuries, where the
instruction to *song kua* — "relax the hips" — took its place alongside
[song](../glossary/song.md) (release) and
[gen](../glossary/gen.md) (rooting) as a foundational cue. It is practical,
anatomical language, refined through generations of embodied teaching.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, the kua is understood as the hinge that transmits force
between the legs and the torso — the point through which the whole body
connects. Classical teaching describes "opening" or "folding" the kua as the
key to letting weight sink and letting movement begin from the center rather
than from the arms. A tight, locked hip is described as a break in the body's
connection; a released kua lets the movement flow.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, kua is simply the **hip joint and surrounding
musculature** — the hip flexors, glutes, and the muscles around the inguinal
crease. "Relaxing the kua" is, in modern terms, releasing tension in the hip
flexors and letting the pelvis settle so the hips can hinge freely. This is
plain biomechanics: a released hip allows the spine to lengthen, the knees to
align over the feet, and force to transmit efficiently from the ground up
through the body. It is the same "hips back and down, don't grip" cue found in
many movement disciplines.

## Related concepts

- **[Song](../glossary/song.md)** — relaxed release; releasing the hips is a
  core expression of it.
- **[Dantian](../glossary/dantian.md)** — the abdominal center that sits just
  above the hips.
- **[Ming Men](../glossary/ming-men.md)** — the lower-back point that opens
  together with a released hip.
- **[Gen](../glossary/gen.md)** — rooting; weight transmits through the hips
  to the ground.
- **[Tai Chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)** — the art whose foundational
  instruction is to relax the kua.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Kua is just the pelvis bone."** It refers to the joint and fold — the
   movable hinge — not merely the bony structure.
2. **"'Relax the kua' means going limp."** It means releasing excess tension
   while staying supported, not collapsing the posture.
3. **"It's an exotic secret."** It is straightforward, trainable hip
   mobility — the same principle behind hip-opening work in many traditions.
