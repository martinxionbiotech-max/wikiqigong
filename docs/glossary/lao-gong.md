# Lao Gong (The Palm Point)

**What it is:** Lao Gong (勞宮, *láo gōng*), "the labor palace," is the point
in the center of the palm — a focal point in tai chi and qigong for attention,
warmth, and the felt sense of the hands.

## Literal meaning

**Lao** (勞) means "labor" or "toil"; **gong** (宮) means "palace" or "hall."
"Labor palace" is the name for the center of the hand — the part that works,
and the place where, in traditional teaching, attention naturally gathers.

## Historical context

Lao gong comes from the acupuncture-point tradition of Chinese medicine,
where it is identified as a point on the pericardium channel, located in the
middle of the palm (traditionally between the second and third metacarpal
bones, roughly where the middle finger lands when the hand curls loosely).
Like other classical points, it was adopted into internal practice as a focus
for attention and breath — a map point, not a modern anatomical structure.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, lao gong is understood as a place where the felt quality of
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) is readily noticed — practitioners describe warmth,
tingling, or a sense of fullness gathering in the palms. In tai chi and
qigong, the hands are often described as sensitive "receivers" and
"expressers" of qi, and attention is frequently rested in the palms. Palms
facing the [dantian](../glossary/dantian.md), or facing each other, are common
postures that use lao gong as a focus.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, lao gong is simply the center of the palm — a place
where attention is easy to place, because the hands are richly supplied with
nerves and blood flow. The warmth and tingling people feel there when they
relax and breathe slowly are ordinary physiological sensations: increased
circulation and relaxed attention. Focusing on the palms is a practical
attention anchor, no different in kind from focusing on the breath or the
feet.

## Related concepts

- **[Qi](../glossary/qi.md)** — the felt quality often sensed in the palms.
- **[Tai Chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)** — the art that keeps attention in the
  hands.
- **[Qigong](../glossary/qigong.md)** — whose palm-centered practices use lao
  gong as a focus.
- **[Meditation](../glossary/meditation.md)** — attention may rest in the
  palms as an anchor.
- **[Dantian](../glossary/dantian.md)** — the common pairing of palms facing
  the lower abdomen.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Lao gong is a magical energy portal."** It is the center of the palm — a
  convenient, sensitive focus for attention, not a supernatural opening.
2. **"You must feel heat to be doing it right."** Warmth in the palms varies
  enormously between people and is not a grade. The practice is the placement
  of attention, not a particular sensation.
3. **"Lao gong is only for energy work."** It is useful simply as a place to
  rest attention, whether or not you use qi language.
