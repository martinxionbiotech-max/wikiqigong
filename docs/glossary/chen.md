# Chen (Sinking)

**What it is:** Chen (沉, *chén*), "sinking," is the downward settling of the
body's weight and the breath — the quality that turns relaxation into
groundedness.

## Literal meaning

**Chen** (沉) means "to sink," "to settle downward," or "to be heavy." In
practice it describes the felt experience of weight dropping into the lower
body and the soles of the feet, and of the breath settling into the lower
abdomen.

## Historical context

Sinking is a recurring instruction in Chinese internal practice, taught
alongside release (*song*, 鬆). Classical tai chi and qigong teaching paired
the two: release the tension (song), and the body sinks (chen); sink, and the
body roots (gen). The idea appears throughout the classical literature of the
internal martial arts as the counterpoint to lifting, bracing, and floating.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, chen is understood as the downward settling of
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) into the [dantian](../glossary/dantian.md) — the lower abdomen — and of
weight into the feet. Teachers describe a body that is "heavy below and light
above," where the shoulders drop, the breath sits low, and the lower body is
stable while the upper body stays free. Sinking is seen as the prerequisite
for rooting and for smooth, powerful movement.

## Modern explanation

Sinking is best read today as the natural physical consequence of relaxation:
when unnecessary tension is released, the body's center of mass lowers, the
shoulders drop, and breathing becomes slower and more abdominal. These are
ordinary, observable changes — the same ones that give relaxed, aligned
standing its stability. Sinking, like rooting, is trainable and testable
without any belief in qi.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Sinking means collapsing or slumping."** Sinking keeps structure; it is
   relaxed settling, not collapse.
2. **"Sinking is a mystical downward energy."** It is the ordinary physical
   result of releasing tension.
3. **"You sink by pushing yourself down."** Sinking is a letting-go, not an
   effort — pushing down recreates the tension you are trying to release.
