# Xing, Yi, Qi (Form, Intention, Energy)

**What it is:** Xing, yi, qi (形 意 氣, *xíng yì qì*) — "form, intention,
energy" — is the three-layer model of how internal practice deepens: from the
outer shape of a movement, through the intention that directs it, to the
subtle quality (qi) that animates it.

## Literal meaning

- **Xing** (形) — "form," "shape," the visible outer structure of a posture or
  movement.
- **Yi** (意) — "intention," "idea," the mind's direction that guides the
  movement.
- **Qi** (氣) — "energy," "breath," the animating quality (see
  [Qi](../glossary/qi.md)).

The three name a progression from the most external to the most internal.

## Historical context

The relationship between form, intention, and qi is a recurring theme in
Chinese internal-arts literature, especially from the Ming and Qing periods
onward. Classical teaching repeated the sequence — first correct the outer
form, then invest it with intention, then let qi follow — though the exact
wording and emphasis vary by school. The model is closely tied to the
"internal harmonies" (內三合, *nèi sān hé*) of the internal martial arts,
which pair mind, intention, and qi with the body's force.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, the three layers are understood as a chain of refinement:
**form** is the container, **intention** is what fills it, and **qi** is what
flows when intention is steady. The classical teaching is that practice moves
*from* form *to* intention *to* qi — the outer shape is learned first, then
the mind is placed in it, and finally the movement becomes light, connected,
and "alive." The saying "where intention goes, qi follows" (意到氣到) captures
the link between the second and third layers.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, the three layers describe an ordinary and
observable learning process:

- **Form** = motor pattern — learning the movement's shape.
- **Intention** = attention — placing the mind in the movement, not doing it
  on autopilot.
- **Qi** = the felt quality that emerges — smoothness, ease, and a sense of
  aliveness — when form and attention align.

In modern terms this is the difference between going through the motions and
practicing with awareness. No belief in qi is required: the third layer is a
felt quality of skilled, attentive movement.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"The three are separate things."** They are layers of one movement —
  form, intention, and quality — not three objects.
2. **"You can skip form and go straight to qi."** The progression matters:
  intention has nothing to inhabit without a correct form first.
3. **"Qi here means a measurable force."** As elsewhere on this site, qi is a
  model for a felt quality, not a physical substance you can detect.
