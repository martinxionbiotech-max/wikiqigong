# Yong Quan (The Bubbling Well)

**What it is:** Yong Quan (湧泉, *yǒng quán*), "gushing spring," is the point
on the sole of the foot — in the soft depression just behind the ball — that
traditional practice uses as the downward reference for grounded, rooted
standing.

## Literal meaning

**Yong** (湧) means "to gush" or "to bubble up"; **quan** (泉) means "spring"
or "fountain." "Gushing spring" names a point pictured as a rising source of
vitality at the foot — the body's lowest point, where contact with the ground
begins.

## Historical context

Yong quan comes from the acupuncture-point tradition of Chinese medicine,
where it is identified as the first point on the *kidney channel* (腎經),
located on the sole of the foot. Like other classical points, it was adopted
into internal practice as a **reference point** rather than an anatomical
structure — traditionally marking the lowest point of the standing body, where
the practitioner's weight meets the earth. It appears most often in
[standing](../glossary/zhan-zhuang.md) and
[rooting](../glossary/gen.md) instruction as a cue for where the weight
settles.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, yong quan is understood as the body's lowest point — the
"wellspring" at the foot through which the standing body connects to the
ground. Classical teaching directs attention to the soles, describing weight
and warmth settling there as the posture roots. The image of a "gushing
spring" points both downward (into the earth) and upward (vitality rising
through the legs), making it a grounding point in the fullest sense.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, yong quan is a way of pointing to the **center of
the foot** — the point through which weight should distribute when standing
well. "Feeling" yong quan is, in modern terms, attending to the soles of the
feet so that weight settles evenly rather than gripping or leaning. This
even distribution of pressure is exactly what supports balance and reduces
excess tension in the legs — the same foot-grounding cue used in dance,
martial arts, and physical therapy, independent of any energy framework.

## Related concepts

- **[Bai Hui](../glossary/bai-hui.md)** — the crown point; sole and crown
  together define the vertical axis.
- **[Gen](../glossary/gen.md)** — rooting; the sole point is where the root
  meets the ground.
- **[Chen](../glossary/chen.md)** — sinking; weight settling downward lands at
  the sole.
- **[Zhan Zhuang](../glossary/zhan-zhuang.md)** — the standing practice whose
  "feel the soles" cue comes from yong quan.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Yong quan is a magical energy valve."** It is a reference point on the
   sole of the foot, used as a cue for weight distribution — not a
   supernatural opening.
2. **"You must push into the ground hard."** The cue is even, relaxed contact,
   not pressing or gripping with the toes.
3. **"It only matters for martial artists."** Even weight distribution through
   the feet supports balance and ease for anyone who stands or walks.
