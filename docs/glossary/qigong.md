# Qigong

**What it is:** Qigong (氣功, *qìgōng*) is a Chinese system of gentle movement,
breathing, and focused attention practiced for health, relaxation, and balance.
The name means, literally, "qi work" or "breath work."

## Literal meaning

**Qi** (氣) is the body's functional energy — breath, warmth, circulation, and
vitality as one system. **Gong** (功) means "work" or "skill developed through
practice" (as in *gongfu*). So qigong is the *deliberate, repeated cultivation*
of qi — not "qi magic," but qi *work*.

## Historical context

The ingredients of qigong — breathing techniques, slow movement, seated
meditation — appear in Chinese sources over two thousand years old, rooted in
Daoist inner alchemy, Chinese medicine, martial arts, and Buddhist meditation.
But the *term* "qigong" in its modern health sense was popularized only in the
**1950s**, when older traditions were standardized for public health. Modern
qigong is thus an ancient *practice* under a modern *label*.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, qigong is understood as the cultivation of qi: building it,
moving it through the body, and clearing blockage. Health is the smooth,
balanced flow of qi; illness is stagnation or deficiency. This is a *model*
for describing health, not a mechanism of cure.

Traditional teaching also divides qigong into four classic families, which
helps make sense of the umbrella term:

| Family | Chinese | Purpose | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Health / medical | 醫療氣功 | Maintain health, support recovery | [Baduanjin](../articles/baduanjin.md) |
| Martial | 武術氣功 | Stability, power, body awareness | [Zhan Zhuang](../glossary/zhan-zhuang.md) |
| Scholarly / meditative | 儒家氣功 | Calm, focus, character | Confucian "quiet sitting" |
| Spiritual / Daoist | 道家氣功 | Inner transformation, longevity | Neidan (inner alchemy) |

Most modern, secular practice draws from the first two families.

## Modern explanation

What qigong demonstrably does is combine slow movement, deep breathing, and
focused attention — three interventions *studied for* their effects on
relaxation, balance, and stress. Practitioners commonly describe warmth,
calm, and a settled feeling, whether or not they use the language of qi. It is
a practice, not a treatment.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Qigong is an ancient unified practice."** The practices are ancient; the
   unified label is mid-20th-century.
2. **"Qigong cures disease."** It is a wellness practice, not medicine.
3. **"Qigong is a religion."** It has philosophical roots but requires no
   belief system.
4. **"Qigong is a martial art."** Some qigong descended from martial
   conditioning, but it does not teach fighting.
