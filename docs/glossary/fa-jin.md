# Fa Jin (Issuing Force)

**What it is:** Fa jin (發勁, *fājìn*) is the sudden, short, whole-body release
of trained force (*jin*) in tai chi — an explosive push or strike delivered
from a relaxed, rooted structure rather than from local muscle effort.

## Literal meaning

**Fa** (發) means "to issue, emit, or release." **Jin** (勁) is "trained
force" — coordinated, refined power (see [jin](../glossary/jin.md)). Together
發勁 is literally **"issuing jin"**: the moment trained force is *released*
outward in a short burst.

## Historical context

The term belongs to the classical vocabulary of the Chinese internal martial
arts. In tai chi it appears in the traditional writings describing how the
body should be "rooted in the feet, developed through the legs, directed by
the waist, and expressed through the fingers" — the ground-up pathway along
which jin travels. Like much of tai chi's technical language, its precise
historical dating is not firmly documented and should be read with the same
uncertainty as the art's own origins (see [tai chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)).

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, fa jin is contrasted with *li* (力, raw muscular strength).
Raw strength is local, tense, and easily resisted; *jin* is whole-body,
relaxed, and sudden. The classical image is of a whip or a wave: force is
generated through the legs and waist and "issued" through a point — the palm,
fist, elbow, or shoulder — in a brief, relaxed burst. The release is a
*turning off* of held tension at the right instant, not a clenching.

## Modern explanation

Read without the traditional language, fa jin is a **biomechanical power
transfer**: force is generated from the ground through the legs, transmitted
through a relaxed but connected torso, and delivered through the point of
contact in a short, coordinated pulse. The "relaxation" is not limpness — it
is the absence of the antagonistic tension that would brake the movement and
slow the transfer. The result looks sudden and effortless precisely because
the power comes from timing and connection, not from size.

## Related concepts

- **[Jin](../glossary/jin.md)** — the trained force that fa jin releases.
- **[Li](../glossary/li.md)** — the raw strength fa jin is distinguished from.
- **[Song](../glossary/song.md)** — the release quality that makes the burst possible.
- **[Rooting (Gen)](../glossary/gen.md)** — the ground connection the force travels through.
- **[Tui Shou](../glossary/tui-shou.md)** — the partner exercise that trains toward it.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Fa jin is a magic or supernatural power."** It is a trainable
   biomechanical skill — coordinated force transfer, not mystery.
2. **"It is hitting as hard as you can."** The point is *trained* force from a
   connected body, not brute strength; tension usually defeats it.
3. **"You must tense up at the moment of release."** The opposite — the release
   comes from *not* tensing, letting coordinated force travel through.
4. **"It is only for fighting."** The underlying skill — relaxed, connected,
   ground-driven movement — is the same quality that makes the slow form
   healthy and balanced.
