# Jingluo (Meridians)

**What it is:** Jingluo (經絡, *jīngluò*), the "meridians," are the network of
channels through which Chinese medicine describes
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) — the body's functional energy — as flowing, linking
the organ systems to the surface of the body.

## Literal meaning

**Jing** (經) originally means the *warp* of a loom — the long, load-bearing
threads — and by extension a "main channel" or "passage." **Luo** (絡) means a
"net" or "network." So 經絡 is literally **"channels and network"**: main
channels connected by a finer web of branches. The English "meridian" is a
20th-century Western translation borrowed from geography (lines of longitude).

## Historical context

The channel concept appears in the *Huangdi Neijing* (黃帝內經, "Yellow
Emperor's Inner Classic"), compiled around the Han dynasty, which describes
channels carrying qi and blood, with points where the flow can be influenced —
the ancestor of acupuncture. Over centuries the map was standardized into
**twelve primary channels** (十二經脈, *shí'èr jīngmài*), each named for an
organ system, plus **eight "extraordinary vessels"** (奇經八脈), including the
midline *du mai* (Governing Vessel) and *ren mai* (Conception Vessel).

## Traditional interpretation

Classically, health is the smooth, balanced flow of qi through the channels;
illness is blockage, deficiency, or misdirection of that flow. The twelve
channels are named for organ systems — lung, heart, stomach, liver, and so on —
but these are **functional systems named after organs**, not anatomical
claims. The "triple burner" channel, for example, names a functional division
of the torso with no single physical counterpart.

## Modern explanation

No anatomical structure matching the meridian map has been found. The most
honest modern reading is that meridians are a **clinical and teaching map** —
a way of organizing the body's relationships into a coherent, teachable
system. Some studies report that acupoints and channel lines correlate in
places with connective-tissue planes and nerve pathways, and acupuncture
*itself* has been studied with mixed, preliminary results — but the "meridian"
as a literal physical channel is not established. It is a model, not a
substance.

## Related concepts

- **[Qi](../glossary/qi.md)** — the energy that flows through the channels.
- **[Qigong](../glossary/qigong.md)** — the practice that works with the
  channel concept.
- **[Dantian](../glossary/dantian.md)** — the lower-abdominal center the
  channels are described as connecting to.
- **[San Guan](../glossary/san-guan.md)** — three points along the back's
  Governing Vessel (*du mai*).

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Meridians are literal tubes or nerves."** No dissected "meridian"
   exists; the map is conceptual, not anatomical.
2. **"Meridians are scientifically proven."** The evidence for a physical
   channel system is not established; "proven meridians" overstates the
   research.
3. **"You must believe in meridians to practice."** Qigong and tai chi work
   as movement, breath, and attention training with or without the map.
4. **"The twelve channels are twelve literal organs."** They are functional
   systems named after organs, a classification scheme rather than anatomy.
