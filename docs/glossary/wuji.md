# Wuji

**What it is:** Wuji (無極, *wújí*) means "without limit" — the still,
undivided state that precedes [yin and yang](../glossary/yin-yang.md), embodied
in practice as the quiet standing posture that begins most tai chi and qigong
forms.

## Literal meaning

**Wu** (無) means "without, not." **Ji** (極) means "limit, extreme," and also
"ridgepole" — the peak beam of a roof. Wuji is thus "the limitless" or "that
which has no ridgepole": the unbounded, undifferentiated whole before anything
splits into parts.

## Historical context

Wuji is a Daoist philosophical term for the boundless and undifferentiated.
It is most famously paired with taiji in the Song-dynasty philosopher **Zhou
Dunyi** (周敦頤, 1017–1073), whose *Taijitu Shuo* (太極圖說, "Explanation of the
Taiji Diagram") opens with "wuji and yet taiji" — placing wuji as the source
from which taiji, and therefore yin and yang, emerge. The term later entered
tai chi's classical literature as the name for the form's opening stillness.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, wuji is the undifferentiated source — the still, empty field
from which all movement and the paired qualities of yin and yang arise. It is
described as a state of no intention, no tension, no direction: the body quiet
and whole before it divides into weight on one leg, breath in or out, movement
left or right.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, wuji is the **neutral starting position**: the body
aligned and at rest, attention gathered, before any action. It is the physical
and mental "zero point" from which tai chi and qigong begin and to which they
return — observable and trainable, with no belief required.

## Related concepts

- **[Yin–Yang](../glossary/yin-yang.md)** — the division that wuji precedes.
- **[Tai Chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)** — the art that begins from wuji.
- **[Song](../glossary/song.md)** — the relaxed-release quality wuji embodies.
- **[Zhan Zhuang](../glossary/zhan-zhuang.md)** — standing meditation that
  extends wuji stillness.
- **[Qi](../glossary/qi.md)** — traditionally understood to gather in the
  still field of wuji.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Wuji is a special power or hidden state."** It is ordinary, quiet,
   aligned standing — a neutral starting position, not an esoteric achievement.
2. **"Wuji is the same as taiji."** They are distinct: wuji is undivided
   stillness; taiji is the balanced whole that holds yin and yang.
3. **"You must be a Daoist to practice wuji."** The posture is purely
   physical and mental — alignment and attention — and requires no belief.
