# Neigong

**What it is:** Neigong (內功, *nèigōng*) means "internal work" or "internal
skill" — the deliberate cultivation of the qualities *inside* movement:
structure, relaxation, breath, and attention.

## Literal meaning

**Nei** (內) means "internal, inner." **Gong** (功) means "work" or "skill
developed through practice." So neigong is the *inner* work that underlies the
*outer* movement — the skill you cannot see from outside.

## Historical context

The term comes from Chinese martial-arts training, where it distinguished
internal conditioning (breath, alignment, awareness) from *waigong* (外功),
"external" conditioning (strength, conditioning, striking). Internal
practices drew heavily on Daoist breathing and meditation traditions, and
neigong became the backbone of the "internal" martial arts, of which tai chi
is the most famous.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, neigong is understood as the cultivation of
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) and the refinement of the "three treasures" —
[Jing](../glossary/jing.md), qi, and [Shen](../glossary/shen.md). The aim is a
body that is relaxed yet connected, rooted yet light, and a mind that is calm
yet alert.

## Modern explanation

Neigong is best read today as **the invisible training layer**: postural
alignment, breath control, and attention. These are the mechanisms behind tai
chi and qigong's benefits — balance, relaxation, body awareness — and they are
observable and testable without any belief in qi.

Strip away the terminology and neigong is three interlocking skills, trained
together:

| Skill | What it is | Where it shows up |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Structure** | Relaxed, aligned posture; rooting | Balance, stability |
| **Breath** | Slow, abdominal breathing | Calm, relaxation |
| **Attention** | Gathered awareness that returns when it drifts | Body awareness, focus |

These are the same three "regulations" as [Qigong](../glossary/qigong.md) —
body, breath, and mind. Neigong and qigong share the same engine; they differ
in emphasis and framing.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Neigong is a secret supernatural power."** It is ordinary, trainable
   body–breath–attention work.
2. **"Neigong and qigong are completely different."** They overlap heavily;
   neigong is the martial-internal framing, qigong the health framing.
3. **"You can learn neigong from a book in weeks."** It is slow, embodied
   work — the "gong" (skill) takes years.
