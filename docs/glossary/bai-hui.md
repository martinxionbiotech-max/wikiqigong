# Bai Hui (The Crown Point)

**What it is:** Bai Hui (百會, *bǎi huì*), "hundred meetings," is the point
at the very top of the head — the crown — that traditional practice uses as an
upward reference point for standing tall, lengthening the spine, and carrying
the head lightly.

## Literal meaning

**Bai** (百) means "hundred"; **hui** (會) means "to meet" or "to gather."
"Hundred meetings" names a point where many channels were traditionally said
to converge — fitting for the highest point of the body's midline, where
everything below appears to gather to a peak.

## Historical context

Bai hui comes from the acupuncture-point tradition of Chinese medicine, where
it is identified as a point on the *Du Mai* (Governing Vessel, 督脈), located
at the top of the head. Like other classical points, it was adopted into
internal practice as a **reference point** rather than an anatomical
structure — traditionally marking the top of the trunk's midline channel, the
upper counterpart to points like [hui yin](../glossary/hui-yin.md) below. It
appears most often in standing and meditation instruction as a cue for the
head's position.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, bai hui is understood as the body's highest point — the "peak"
toward which the upright posture lengthens. Classical teaching describes a
gentle upward lift at the crown, often pictured as the head being suspended by
a thread from above, so the spine hangs long and free. This is paired with
sinking below: the crown lifts lightly while the weight settles downward, a
single vertical line from top to bottom.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, bai hui is a way of pointing to **upright head
posture** — the crown balanced directly over the spine rather than jutting
forward or tilting back. "Lifting" bai hui is, in modern terms, gently
lengthening the back of the neck and tucking the chin slightly, which brings
the head into neutral alignment over the shoulders. This reduces strain on the
neck and upper back and lets the spine elongate — the same postural cue used
in many movement and exercise systems, independent of any energy framework.

- **[Xiao Zhou Tian](../glossary/xiao-zhou-tian.md)** — the microcosmic orbit,

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Bai hui is a magical energy portal."** It is a reference point at the
   top of the head, used as a cue for head posture — not a supernatural
   opening.
2. **"You must push the head up hard."** The cue is a gentle lift, not a rigid
   upward stretch. Forcing it can tense the neck.
3. **"It only matters for advanced practice."** Neutral head alignment reduces
   neck and upper-back strain for anyone, at any level.
