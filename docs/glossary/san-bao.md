# San Bao (Three Treasures)

**What it is:** The Three Treasures (三寶, *sān bǎo*) — Jing (精), Qi (氣), and
Shen (神) — is the classical framework that maps the human being as a single
continuum from dense matter to refined mind.

## Literal meaning

**San** (三) is "three." **Bao** (寶) is "treasure." The "three treasures" are
the three most precious resources of a human life: Jing, Qi, and Shen.

## Historical context

The three-treasures idea is central to Daoist inner-alchemy
(*neidan*, 內丹) tradition, which described spiritual development as the
stepwise refinement of jing into qi, and qi into shen — often rendered
"refining the essence to transmute it into energy, and the energy to transmute
it into spirit." The framework was later absorbed into Chinese medicine and
the internal martial arts.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, the three treasures form a hierarchy of increasing subtlety:

| Treasure | Chinese | Domain |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Jing (精) | essence | body, vitality, constitution |
| Qi (氣) | breath/energy | movement, circulation, aliveness |
| Shen (神) | spirit | mind, awareness, presence |

Cultivation aims to preserve jing, regulate qi, and calm shen — keeping the
three in balance. The classical chain runs **jing → qi → shen**: essence
refines into energy, energy into spirit. But the three are trained together,
not in strict sequence — the "stages" are a teaching model.

## Modern explanation

The three treasures are best read as a **pre-scientific model of the
mind–body continuum**: jing ≈ the physical/structural, qi ≈ the functional
energy and breath, shen ≈ consciousness. It is a reminder, in modern terms,
that body, energy, and mind are not separate — which aligns with modern
mind–body research.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"The three treasures are literal substances."** They are categories for
   layers of a person, not physical objects you can point to.
2. **"They are only religious."** The framework is also used secularly in
   Chinese medicine and martial arts.
3. **"You must master jing before qi before shen."** The three are
   interdependent and trained together; the "stages" are a teaching model,
   not a strict sequence.
