# Fang Song (Release & Loosen)

**What it is:** Fang Song (放鬆, *fàng sōng*), "to release and loosen," is the
everyday Chinese word for "relax" — and the explicit, two-character form of
the instruction that tai chi's [song](../glossary/song.md) (鬆) points to.

## Literal meaning

- **Fang** (放) — "to release, let go, set free."
- **Song** (鬆) — "loose, relaxed, to loosen."

Together, *fang song* means "to release and loosen" — to actively let go of
held tension, stated as a verb rather than a state.

## Historical context

*Fang song* is the common modern Chinese word for "relax," and it is the
everyday phrase used in tai chi and qigong classes throughout the
Chinese-speaking world. It carries the same root character — *song* (鬆) — as
the classical term from the "Tai Chi Classics," where relaxed openness is
named as the precondition for qi circulation and rootedness. Where the
classical texts use the single character *song*, modern teaching most often
gives the instruction in the fuller, active form: *fàng sōng* — "release."

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, release is understood as the gate through which everything else
follows: a tense body is said to block [qi](../glossary/qi.md) and to make
strength local and stiff, while a released body lets weight settle and lets
whole-body [jin](../glossary/jin.md) emerge. *Fang song* is the instruction
that produces the state of *song* — you cannot "be" loose by force; you can
only *release* into it.

The key distinction, as with *song*, is three-way:

| State | Quality |
|-------|---------|
| **Tense** | braced, held, blocked |
| **Fang song** | released, open, responsive — *with structure* |
| **Collapsed** | limp, slack, structureless |

Release is the middle state — soft but not limp.

## Modern explanation

Read without metaphysics, *fang song* is simply **progressive relaxation with
structure kept intact** — the same skill taught in many movement, athletic,
and stress-reduction systems. It is observable and trainable: on the
instruction to "release," the shoulders drop, the jaw softens, the breath
deepens, and unnecessary muscular holding lets go while posture is maintained.
This is the mechanism behind much of tai chi's studied effect on tension and
stress.

## Related concepts

- **[Song](../glossary/song.md)** — the state that *fang song* (the action)
  produces.
- **[Chen](../glossary/chen.md)** — sinking; released weight settles downward
  into the feet.
- **[Tai Chi](../glossary/tai-chi.md)** — the art whose first and most
  repeated cue this is.
- **[Zhan Zhuang](../glossary/zhan-zhuang.md)** — the standing practice that
  most directly trains release.
- **[Breathing](../glossary/breathing.md)** — the breath that deepens as the
  body releases.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Fang song means going limp."** It means *releasing unnecessary* tension
   while keeping structure — not collapsing.
2. **"You can think yourself relaxed."** Release is embodied and trained —
   felt in the shoulders, jaw, and breath — not decided intellectually.
3. **"Fang song and song are different concepts."** They are the same root
   idea: *fang song* is the active, everyday wording; *song* is the classical
   single character.
