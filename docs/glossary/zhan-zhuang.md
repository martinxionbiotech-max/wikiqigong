# Zhan Zhuang

**What it is:** Zhan Zhuang (站樁, *zhàn zhuāng*) is standing meditation — the
practice of holding a relaxed, aligned, rooted posture for extended periods,
to build body awareness and internal stability.

## Literal meaning

**Zhan** (站) means "to stand." **Zhuang** (樁) means "post" or "stake" — the
kind driven into the ground. The name is an instruction: *stand like a post* —
upright, rooted, unmoving, but alive.

## Historical context

Standing postures appear in the internal-martial-arts traditions as the
foundation of training: before learning to move, a student learns to stand.
Zhan zhuang was systematized as a health and martial practice most famously
by **Wang Xiangzhai** in the early 20th century, who taught it as a complete
system of internal cultivation in itself.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, zhan zhuang is understood as the most direct way to build and
circulate [qi](../glossary/qi.md): standing still, with breath settled in the
[dantian](../glossary/dantian.md), lets the body's energy gather and the
structure relax into alignment. It is the still root from which movement
grows.

## Modern explanation

Standing still in a relaxed, aligned posture is, in effect, low-intensity
isometric training of posture and balance — and sustained, gentle attention on
the body *may help* body awareness and relaxation. It is the simplest form of
the three regulations of [qigong](../glossary/qigong.md), done standing.

A beginner's zhan zhuang is simple and low:

1. **Stand** feet shoulder-width, knees soft, spine long, crown lifting gently.
2. **Scan** the body — shoulders, jaw, neck, chest, belly — and notice tension.
3. **Release, don't collapse** — soften each place while keeping structure.
4. **Breathe** into the release, letting the exhale carry the letting-go.

Low horse stances are advanced; beginners stand high and relaxed.

## Common misunderstandings

1. **"Standing still is easy."** Holding a relaxed, aligned posture for
   minutes is genuinely demanding — and the effort is the point.
2. **"You must stand in a low horse stance."** Beginners stand with feet
   shoulder-width and knees only slightly bent. Low stances are advanced.
3. **"It's a magical energy practice."** It is ordinary posture and attention
   training, with observable effects on stability and awareness.
