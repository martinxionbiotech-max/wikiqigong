# About WikiQigong

**WikiQigong is a learning resource, not a content farm.** We exist to close a
specific gap: the distance between the deep, precise Chinese understanding of
tai chi, qigong, and the mind–body arts — and the simplified, sometimes
mystified English versions that circulate online.

We take genuine Chinese concepts and translate them into **natural, accurate
English** — keeping the characters, the pinyin, and the honest history — so a
reader with zero Chinese background can understand them, and a practitioner
who wants the real thing gets it without the exoticism.

---

## Why this site exists

Chinese culture has spent more than two thousand years thinking carefully about
how body, breath, and mind work together. That knowledge is practical,
internally consistent, and valuable. It is also frequently misrepresented in
two opposite directions:

- **Mystified** — dressed up as supernatural "energy" or secret power, which
  misleads beginners and discourages skeptical readers.
- **Stripped** — translated so loosely that the Chinese context, and therefore
  much of the meaning, is lost.

WikiQigong is the middle path. We preserve the Chinese context *and* give a
grounded, non-mystical reading. Both halves matter, and every page does both.

## Who we are

WikiQigong is written and reviewed by the **WikiQigong Editorial Team** — a
small group working to produce clear, accurate, well-sourced educational
material. We are not medical professionals, and we do not pretend to be. Where
a topic touches medicine, diagnosis, or treatment, we say so plainly and point
to authoritative sources rather than speaking beyond our expertise.

We publish under an editorial policy (see [Editorial
Standards](editorial-standards.md)) that commits us to:

- **Accuracy about Chinese culture** — correct characters, correct pinyin with
  tone marks, and honest dating ("compiled around the Han dynasty," not
  "ancient secrets").
- **Honesty about evidence** — we separate historical fact, traditional
  interpretation, and modern scientific reading, and we hedge health claims
  carefully.
- **Traceability** — every research claim carries a real, checkable source.
  Where the evidence is thin, we say it is thin.

---

## What this site is — and is not

**It is:**

- An educational resource on Chinese mind–body practice.
- A glossary and knowledge graph of the core concepts.
- A practical guide, with beginner sessions and safety notes.

**It is not:**

- **Medical advice.** Read the full [Medical Disclaimer](disclaimer.md). No
  practice described here cures, treats, or prevents disease.
- **A religion.** These practices have Daoist and Buddhist roots, but the
  modern health forms require no belief system.
- **Machine translation.** Every page is deliberate reconstruction of meaning,
  not word-for-word rendering.

---

## How to trust what you read here

Three questions we encourage every reader to ask of any page:

1. **Is the history honest?** If a date is uncertain, we say so. We never
   invent dates, texts, or lineages.
2. **Are the health claims hedged?** We use "may support," "has been studied
   for," and "research suggests" — never "cure," "treat," or "proven."
3. **Can I verify the sources?** We cite real studies and authoritative
   institutions (NCCIH, PubMed-indexed research, Harvard Health) so you can
   check, not just trust.
