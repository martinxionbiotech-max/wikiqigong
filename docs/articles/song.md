# Song: The Art of Relaxed Release

> **TL;DR** — Song (鬆, *sōng*) means "relaxed release" — the foundational
> quality of tai chi and qigong: soft, open, and free of unnecessary tension,
> *without* collapsing. It is the single most repeated instruction in tai chi,
> and the quality everything else depends on.

If you learn only one word of Chinese for your practice, make it **song**. It
is the first thing every teacher says, the quality every technique serves, and
the thing beginners most often misunderstand. This page explains what it
really means and how to cultivate it.

## What song means

Song (鬆) is usually translated "relax," but the translation is too weak. It
means **relaxed release** — the state of being soft and open, free of
unnecessary tension, *while still holding structure*.

The key distinction:

| State | Quality |
|-------|---------|
| **Rigid** | tense, braced, blocked |
| **Song** | soft, open, responsive — *with structure* |
| **Collapsed** | limp, slack, structureless |

Song is the middle state — the one that is hardest to find and most valuable
to keep.

---

## The three registers

### Historical fact

Song has been a central instruction in the Chinese internal arts for
centuries. The classical tai chi texts — the poems and treatises attributed to
figures like Zhang Sanfeng and Wang Zongyue, whose precise authorship is
debated — return again and again to the same injunctions: relax the waist,
sink the shoulders, drop the elbows, and never use stiff force. These texts
were compiled roughly from the Ming to the Qing dynasty, and "song" in them is
a technical term, not a casual suggestion.

### Traditional interpretation

Traditionally, song is understood as the condition that lets
[qi](../glossary/qi.md) flow freely. A tense body, in this view, "blocks" qi;
a song body "lets it through." Classical teaching places song first: without
song there is no sinking, no rooting, no smooth movement — the other qualities
are said to follow from it.

### Modern explanation

Read in modern terms, song is the deliberate reduction of *unnecessary*
muscular tension while keeping postural structure intact. This is directly
observable: relaxed, aligned movement is more efficient, less fatiguing, and
more responsive than braced, tense movement. Song is, in effect, the skill of
moving without holding — a trainable motor habit, not a mystical state.

## Why song is the foundation

In tai chi and qigong, almost everything rests on song:

- **Movement** — a tense body moves stiffly; a song body moves smoothly and
  continuously.
- **Breathing** — tension in the chest and shoulders restricts the breath;
  song lets the breath deepen (see [Breathing](../glossary/breathing.md)).
- **Balance** — a braced body is rigid and easy to unbalance; a song body is
  stable and responsive (see [Balance](../glossary/balance.md)).
- **Rooting** — relaxed weight settles down into the feet; tension keeps it
  stuck up in the shoulders.

Traditional teaching puts it bluntly: a tense body blocks
[qi](../glossary/qi.md); a song body lets it flow. In modern terms: tension
blocks relaxed, efficient movement; song enables it.

---

## Song and "sinking"

Song is traditionally paired with **"sinking"** (*chen*, 沉). When you release
unnecessary tension, your weight naturally settles downward — shoulders drop,
breath sinks to the belly, and the feet feel heavier and more grounded.

This is not a mystical idea. It is simply what happens when you stop holding
yourself up against gravity: the body finds its natural alignment, and
stability follows.

## How to cultivate song

The most direct way to develop song is through [zhan
zhuang](../articles/zhan-zhuang.md), standing meditation:

1. **Stand relaxed and aligned** — feet shoulder-width, knees soft, spine
   long, crown lifting gently.
2. **Scan the body** — shoulders, jaw, neck, chest, belly, hands. Notice
   where you are holding tension.
3. **Release, don't collapse** — let each place soften while keeping your
   structure upright. Imagine tension melting downward into the ground.
4. **Breathe into the release** — let the exhale carry the letting-go.

Do this for even a few minutes and the quality of song becomes tangible — a
sensation of soft, grounded readiness.

---

## Common mistakes

1. **Going limp.** Releasing tension is not collapsing. If you slump, you
   lost structure — re-lengthen, then release again.
2. **Forcing it.** You cannot strain your way to relaxation. Song is a
   letting-go, not an effort.
3. **Thinking instead of feeling.** Song is embodied. You learn it in the
   body, through standing and slow movement, not by reasoning.

## Who should be cautious

Song itself is gentle — it is the *absence* of strain. But a note for the
honest practitioner:

- If you have joint, back, or balance issues, work within a pain-free range
  and practice near support.
- If "relaxing" turns into collapsing, re-establish your alignment first —
  slumping is not song, and it can strain the lower back over time.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does song mean?**
Song (鬆) means "relaxed release" — the body soft, open, and free of
unnecessary tension, while still holding structure. It is the opposite of both
rigidity and collapse.

**Is song the same as being limp?**
No. Song releases *unnecessary* tension while keeping alignment and readiness.
Going limp collapses the structure; song releases it.

**Why is song so important in tai chi?**
Because tai chi's qualities — softness, responsiveness, rootedness, and
relaxation — all depend on it. A tense body cannot move smoothly or respond
freely.

**How do I practice song?**
Start with zhan zhuang (standing meditation): stand relaxed and aligned, and
gradually let go of tension in the shoulders, jaw, and breath. Notice — and
release — without collapsing.

**Is song only for martial arts?**
No. It is the core of all internal practice, and of any relaxed movement.
Releasing unnecessary tension is universally useful.

**Can I "think" my way to song?**
No. Song is a felt, embodied quality trained through standing and slow
movement — not an idea you can reason yourself into.
