# A Timeline of Tai Chi & Qigong

> **TL;DR** — Chinese mind–body practice is not one thing with one birthday.
> The health arts (daoyin, *tuna* breathing) are **2,000+ years old**, with
> the earliest visual evidence sealed in a tomb around **168 BCE**. Tai chi as
> a named martial art is a **17th-century** development, and the standardized
> **24-form** most people learn today dates only from **1956**. Qigong as a
> single modern label is even younger — mid-20th century.

Most timelines you will find blur these together. This one keeps them separate:
**the ancient health arts**, **the emergence of tai chi**, and **the modern
standardization** that created the practices taught in parks and studios
today.

---

## Why a timeline helps

The confusion is structural. "Qigong" and "tai chi" sound like timeless
traditions, but each layer has a different origin and a different kind of
evidence:

| Layer | Rough start | Evidence quality |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| Therapeutic movement & breath (daoyin, tuna) | ~2nd century BCE and earlier | Good — physical artifacts |
| Named internal martial art (tai chi) | 17th century | Good — family records, texts |
| Modern "qigong" as an umbrella term | mid-20th century | Good — documented |
| Standardized forms (24-form, etc.) | 1956 onward | Exact — official records |

A reader who knows this table can no longer be sold "a 5,000-year-old secret
tai chi form." The truth is more interesting — and more useful.

## The timeline

### ~2nd century BCE — the earliest pictures

The **Mawangdui** (馬王堆) tomb, sealed around 168 BCE in Changsha, contained a
silk scroll depicting **daoyin** (導引) — figures in therapeutic postures,
stretching and breathing. This is the earliest surviving visual record of the
movement-and-breath tradition that would later feed into qigong. See
[Dao Yin](articles/dao-yin.md).

### ~1st–4th century — texts and medicine

The **Huangdi Neijing** (黃帝內經, "Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon"), compiled over
centuries and conventionally dated to the Han dynasty, links breath, movement,
and health — the conceptual foundation of later practice. Daoist traditions
developed breathing methods (*tuna*, 吐納) alongside it.

### 6th century — animal forms formalized

The **Wu Qin Xi** (五禽戲, "Five Animal Frolics") is traditionally attributed to
the physician **Hua Tuo** (華佗, ~145–208 CE), and appears in later medical
texts. Whether or not Hua Tuo authored it, the set of animal-mimicking
exercises became one of the oldest named routines. See
[Wu Qin Xi](articles/wu-qin-xi.md).

### 6th century — the muscle/tendon classic

**Yi Jin Jing** (易筋經, "Muscle/Tendon Change Classic") is traditionally
attributed to the semi-legendary **Bodhidharma** (達摩), a claim historians
treat with caution; the text as we have it is later. See
[Yi Jin Jing](articles/yi-jin-jing.md).

### ~11th century — the philosophical diagram

The Song-dynasty philosopher **Zhou Dunyi** (周敦頤, 1017–1073) wrote the
*Taijitu Shuo*, placing **wuji** (無極) as the source from which **taiji**
(太極) and yin–yang emerge — the philosophical frame tai chi would later borrow.
See [Wuji](glossary/wuji.md).

### 17th century — tai chi emerges as a martial art

Tai chi (太極拳) as a named martial art is associated with the **Chen** family
of Chenjiagou, Henan, and traditionally with **Chen Wangting**. This is where
the art separates from the older health traditions and becomes a fighting
system with internal principles. See [What Is Tai Chi?](articles/what-is-tai-chi.md).

### 19th century — Yang style and spread

**Yang Luchan** (楊露禪, 1799–1872) learned the Chen family art and adapted it
into the softer, more accessible **Yang style**. From Yang branched the
**Wu (吳)** and **Wu (武)/Hao** styles; **Sun Lutang** created the newest style,
**Sun**, in the early 20th century. See
[Tai Chi Styles](glossary/tai-chi-styles.md).

### 1949 — "qigong" becomes the umbrella term

The modern word **qigong** (氣功) was popularized as a single label covering
the many older movement, breathing, and meditation traditions — partly for
public-health promotion. The practices are ancient; the one-word label is
mid-century.

### 1956 — the 24-form

China's sports authorities commissioned the **24-form** (二十四式), a
standardized, shortened Yang-style routine, to make tai chi teachable to
ordinary people. It is now the most practiced tai chi form in the world. See
[Tai Chi 24-Form](articles/tai-chi-24-form.md).

### 1990s–present — the research era

From the late 20th century, tai chi and qigong became subjects of peer-reviewed
research — most prominently the 2005 Li et al. randomized controlled trial on
falls in older adults. See
[Tai Chi for Older Adults](articles/tai-chi-for-older-adults.md).

## What this timeline is *not*

- **Not a claim of unbroken lineage.** The practices evolved, cross-pollinated,
  and were reinvented. Gaps and uncertainties are real and marked.
- **Not a claim that "qigong is 5,000 years old."** The *concepts* are ancient;
  the *label* and the modern *forms* are not.
- **Not a claim that tai chi is a martial secret.** Its 20th-century
  transformation into a mass health practice is well documented.

## Frequently asked questions

**How old is qigong?**
The practices that became qigong are at least 2,000 years old. The earliest
surviving visual evidence is the Mawangdui daoyin scrolls, unearthed from a
tomb sealed around 168 BCE, which show therapeutic stretching and breathing
postures.

**How old is tai chi?**
Tai chi as a named martial art is generally dated from the 17th century,
associated with the Chen family of Chenjiagou, Henan. The standardized 24-form
that made it a mass practice dates from 1956.

**Did qigong come before tai chi?**
Yes. The therapeutic movement-and-breath traditions (daoyin, tuna) that qigong
descends from are far older than tai chi, which emerged as a martial art in the
17th century and later absorbed those health methods.

**When was the word 'qigong' coined?**
The modern umbrella term "qigong" (氣功) was popularized in the mid-20th
century, though the character pairing appears earlier. The practices it names
are ancient; the single label is modern.

**When was the Tai Chi 24-form created?**
In 1956, commissioned by China's sports authorities to provide a short,
standardized introductory form based on the Yang style. It is now the most
widely practiced tai chi form in the world.

**Is the timeline exact?**
No. Many early dates are approximate and rely on traditional attributions.
This timeline distinguishes well-documented dates from traditional ones, and
flags uncertainty rather than hiding it.
